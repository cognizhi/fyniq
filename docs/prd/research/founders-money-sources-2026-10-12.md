# Source notes: founders' balances and conversion to share capital

Supports PRD-001 §8.22 (revision 0.3). An AI-assisted research pass collected these notes on
12 Oct 2026, from official sources where they exist. They are general information, not legal or
tax advice. The accountant and the tax agent confirm each point before the founders rely on it.
SSO links have the form `https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/CoA1967?ProvIds=prNN-`.

## Verdicts

| # | Question | Finding | Source (date) | Short quote |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Return of allotment deadline | **Corrected.** A private company has no 14-day deadline. The allotment is made by lodging the return, and it takes effect only when ACRA updates the electronic register of members, so it cannot be backdated. The 14-day deadline (s63A) is for public companies. For shares not paid in cash, lodge the written contract (or a certified copy) with the return; if there is no written contract, lodge a statement of particulars (s63B). ACRA may ask for the original "duly stamped". | Companies Act s63, s63A, s63B; ACRA "Allotment of shares" (30 Apr 2026) | "does not take effect until the electronic register of members of the company is updated by the Registrar" |
| 2 | Approval to issue shares | Directors need the company's prior approval in general meeting (s161). An ordinary resolution is enough unless the constitution says otherwise, and a private company may pass it as a written resolution (s184A). The approval must be lodged with ACRA within 14 days (s161(5) applies s186). It lapses at the next AGM. Shares issued without it are void (s161(6)). | Companies Act s161, s184A, s186 | "the directors must not, without the prior approval of the company in general meeting, exercise any power of the company to issue shares" |
| 3 | Par value | **Verified.** Shares have no par value (s62A). Any share premium account became share capital on 30 Jan 2006 (s62B). | Companies Act s62A, s62B | "Shares of a company have no par or nominal value." |
| 4 | Loans to directors | **Verified.** s162 bans loans, quasi-loans, guarantees, security and credit transactions for directors, including their spouse and children. Exempt private companies are not covered by the ban. An exempt private company has no corporate shareholder and at most 20 members (s4(1)). Advances for company expenses are allowed with prior approval in general meeting, or if repaid within 6 months after the next AGM (s162(3)(a), (4)). | Companies Act s162, s4 | "a company (other than an exempt private company) must not make a restricted transaction" |
| 5 | Stamp duty on new shares | Share duty applies to transfer documents. No IRAS page says a new allotment is exempt; a secondary source says no duty is charged on new issues. Whether a debt-conversion agreement is dutiable is **not confirmed**. | IRAS "Basics of stamp duty for shares" (14 Aug 2026); ACCA Singapore (secondary) | ACCA: "no stamp duty on the issuance of new shares" |
| 6 | When pay is taxed | **Verified.** Salary is taxed in the year it is due, whether paid or not. Director's fees are taxed when the director becomes entitled to them: for fees approved in arrears, the date of the meeting that votes them. **Shares in lieu of pay: no IRAS guidance found.** Inference: the pay was taxed when due, and shares worth more than the debt released may be a further gain under the employee share rules. | IRAS IR8A explanatory notes YA2026 (7 Aug 2026); IRAS e-Tax guides (30 Jan 2026) | "Report the amount due for the year 2025 regardless of whether: It was paid in the year 2025" |
| 7 | Company deduction for unpaid pay | **Partly verified.** An expense is deductible when the liability is incurred (s14(1)). IRAS sets limits for director's fees approved in arrears (voted at the relevant AGM) and for non-contractual bonuses. No rule was found for contractual salary that stays unpaid. A company gets no deduction for **new** shares it issues to employees. | e-Tax guide "Director's Fees and Bonuses" (30 Jan 2026); e-Tax guide on employee equity-based remuneration (30 Sep 2025) | "No tax deduction is allowed if new shares are issued for the purpose of the transfer." |
| 8 | Loan waiver, company tax | **Partly verified.** IRAS ruled that waiving a non-trade working-capital loan was a capital transaction and not taxable; the ruling binds only that applicant. No general guidance was found for waiving amounts the company deducted earlier. | IRAS Advance Ruling Summary 15/2026 (1 Sep 2026) | "The intended waiver of the loan by Lender is a capital transaction" |
| 9 | Pre-commencement costs | **Corrected:** the section is s14R (not s14U). The business is deemed to start on the first day of the accounting year in which it earns its first dollar. Revenue costs from up to 12 months before that date are deductible. Incorporation costs are capital. Founders' revenue costs from before incorporation: **not found**. | IRAS e-Tax guide on pre-commencement expenses (30 Jan 2026) | "revenue expenses incurred up to 12 months prior to the deemed date of commencement" |
| 10 | GST on costs a founder paid | **Verified.** An invoice in an employee's or director's name is not claimable unless the company can prove the person acted as its agent. Costs from before incorporation are claimable in the first GST return with IRAS's approval when all of these hold: the payer became a member, officer or employee; was reimbursed or holds a written undertaking; was not GST-registered; and bought only for the business. The pre-registration rules must also hold: services within 6 months, goods still held. | IRAS GST pages (13 Jun 2025; 3 Jun 2026) | "You are not allowed to claim input tax for purchases if the tax invoices are addressed to your employee" |
| 11 | CPF on deferred pay and fees | **Corrected wording.** Contributions are due on the last day of the month. Enforcement starts after the 14th of the next month, with interest of 1.5% a month. CPF applies to wages "due or granted" (CPF Act s2). Inference: deferring the cash does not defer CPF; no CPF page on unpaid director salary was found. No CPF on director's fees voted at a general meeting, or on shares in kind. | CPF Board (5 Aug 2026; FAQ 7 Jun 2024); CPF Act s2, s7 | "CPF contributions are not payable on directors' fees voted to them at General Meetings" |
| 12 | Debt-for-equity accounting | **Verified.** Equity issued to extinguish a liability is measured at its fair value, and the difference goes to profit or loss (IFRIC 19 ¶6–9). Creditors acting as shareholders are excluded (¶3(a)). SFRS for Small Entities has the same rule, with the same exclusion (¶22.15A–C, 2024 edition). Unlisted companies use FRS by default. SFRS for Small Entities is optional for companies that are not publicly accountable and meet 2 of 3 tests: revenue ≤ S$10m, assets ≤ S$10m, ≤ 50 employees. The 3rd edition applies from 1 Jan 2027; whether its paragraph numbers change was not checked. | IFRS Foundation, IFRIC 19 (2026 ed.); ACRA, SFRS for Small Entities (2024) | "the creditor is also a direct or indirect shareholder and is acting in its capacity as a direct or indirect existing shareholder" |
| 13 | Shares for services | **Verified.** Equity-settled share-based payment: services are measured at the grant-date fair value of the equity (IFRS 2 ¶10–11; SFRS for Small Entities 26.7–26.8). Inference: settling pay already accrued is an extinguishment of that liability (item 12), not a new share-based payment. | IFRS 2 (2026 ed.); SFRS for Small Entities | "measure the fair value of the services received by reference to the fair value of the equity instruments granted" |
| 14 | Waiver credited to equity | **Verified as a principle; needs judgement.** Contributions from owners are not income (SFRS for Small Entities 2.23(a)). Whether the creditor acted as an owner is a judgement (IFRIC 19 BC7). The credit goes to a capital-contribution reserve, not to share capital, because no shares are issued. | SFRS for Small Entities 2.23; IFRIC 19 BC7 | "would be a matter of judgement depending on the facts and circumstances" |

## Practice notes

- **Startup SG Founder** is still running (page updated 20 Aug 2026). The grant is S$20k–S$50k,
  matched 1:1 by the founders' paid-up capital. At least half of the match must be paid-up capital
  on ACRA when the founders apply. First-time founders must hold at least 30% of the equity. The
  grant "must not be used to pay off loans taken by the startup and its founder(s)". Not
  confirmed: whether a capitalised founder loan counts as injected capital (ask Enterprise
  Singapore).
- **Investors.** Only US and UK sources were found, so treat this as general practice, not
  Singapore practice. Founder loans are disclosed in due diligence. Repaying them from round
  proceeds is rare; they are usually repaid later or converted in the round. Investors often
  require large deferred salaries to be waived or converted before they invest.
- Many Singapore corporate-secretarial websites still say a private company's return of allotment
  is due "within 14 days". That is out of date (item 1).

  amounts the company deducted earlier; founders' revenue costs from before incorporation.

- Companies Act changes from the tranche commencing 20 Oct 2026: ACRA's summary lists no change to
