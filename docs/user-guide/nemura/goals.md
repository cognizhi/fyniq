# Set your goals and update their key results

## What it is

**My goals** shows the company's and the teams' goals, your own goals with their key results, and the KPIs your manager set for your role. A key result is a number that moves from a start value to a target, such as pilot customers live from 0 to 3, or response time from 480 ms down to 300 ms. Progress is worked out exactly and rounded down, so a goal is never shown more done than it is, and the status says whether it keeps up with the time that has passed: done, on track, behind, or not yet for a KPI with no period.

## Before you start

- The company switches on **Performance** under **Settings, Features**.
- Everyone reads the company's and the teams' goals. Only you, your line manager and the people team read your own goals and KPIs.

## Steps

1. Under **Add a goal**, write the goal and its due date. Choose a company or team goal it **aligns with** if there is one. Add a key result with its unit and target; enter a target below the start value when lower is better.
2. Under each key result, type the value now and select **Update**. The history keeps who gave each value and when; the latest value is the one shown.
3. Select **Mark done** when the goal is reached. A goal is done or cancelled once, and a closed goal's key results are no longer updated.
4. Your manager or the HR administrator can set your role KPIs and, on your behalf, goals; you update the value of your own KPI.

## What happens next

- Company goals are set by the HR administrator or the Owner; team goals by a line manager, the HR administrator or the Owner.
- Nemura shows no company financial figure. A KPI that is read from Fyniq's metrics arrives with the link to Fyniq; until then values are entered by a person.

## Common problems

- **It says there is no such goal or person for you.** You may not read or change it: only the person, their line manager and the people team read a personal goal.
- **The target is on the wrong side of the start value.** Choose a target to move towards: below the start value when lower is better.
- **The value is refused.** Use a decimal number with at most four decimals, such as 2 or 12.5.
