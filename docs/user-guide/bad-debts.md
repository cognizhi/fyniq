# Bad debts and GST relief

## What it is

When an invoice that bore GST is written off as a bad debt, the GST can be claimed back from IRAS in the GST return. **Sales › Bad debts** lists each such write-off with the GST in the
part written off, the first day a claim may be made and the last, and where it stands.

## The dates

- A claim may be made once twelve months have passed from the date of supply, or from the write-off when the debtor became insolvent sooner.
- It must be made within five years from the date of supply (seven for a supply before 1 January 2007), the last day being the day before the anniversary; the accountant confirms this.
- **Not yet**, **Claim now** and **Out of time** say where each debt stands. The dates come from the statutory pack, never from the page.

The GST in the part written off is the invoice's GST in proportion to the amount written off, to the cent, half away from zero; the accountant confirms the rounding.

## Claiming the relief

On a row that says **Claim now**, **Claim relief** opens a form. Give the day you claim (within the dates above, and not after today), the last day of the GST return period the claim goes in, and a note: the steps you took to recover the debt, or the debtor's insolvency. The conditions that are not dates are your declaration: that you took reasonable steps to recover the debt, and that the price was no more than the open market value. The tax agent, the accountant and the finance controller can claim.

The claim posts at once. It takes the invoice's GST in the part written off out of the GST control account and puts it against the account the debt was written off to, in Singapore dollars. The amount is worked out on the server and shown on the row once claimed. Each write-off is claimed once; an invoice that a credit note credits cannot be claimed here, because the credit note adjusted its GST.

If a claim was wrong, void it (it is reversed, never deleted) and claim again. A write-off that has a claim cannot be reversed until its claim is voided. A claim made after the debt is later paid, and an insolvent debtor's date, are not here yet: ask the accountant.
