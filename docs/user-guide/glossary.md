# Glossary

The words Fyniq uses for money and the books, in plain words. Where a page uses one of them, it is
underlined with dots: point at it or move to it with Tab to read what it means here.

## Accounts payable

What the company owes its suppliers for bills it has not paid yet. Bills to pay are listed under
**Purchases**.

## Accounts receivable

What customers owe the company for invoices they have not paid yet. Each customer's statement shows
theirs.

## Accrual

Recording income or a cost in the month it belongs to, even when the money moves in another month.
For example, December's electricity, billed in January, is a December cost.

## Balance sheet

What the company has and what it owes at a date, and the owners' share: what is left when what it
owes is taken from what it has.

## Close

Checking a month's books and then locking the month, so nothing changes it. A closed month takes no
new entry; changing it means reopening it, which another person approves.

## Credit note

A document that takes an amount off an invoice: off what a customer owes, or, from a supplier, off
what the company owes them.

## Debit and credit

The two sides of every entry in the books. Every entry's debits equal its credits, so the books
always balance. Money coming into a bank account is a debit to that account; the account it is for,
such as sales, takes the credit.

## Deferred revenue

Money billed for a service the company has not provided yet. It becomes revenue month by month, as
the service is provided.

## Dimension

A tag on a line, such as a department or a project, so its revenue or cost can be reported by it.

## GST

Singapore's Goods and Services Tax. A GST-registered company charges it on what it sells and claims
back what it paid on what it buys; the difference goes to IRAS with each GST return.

## Journal

An entry made directly in the books, as debits and credits, for something no invoice, bill or bank
line records, such as a correction or a year-end adjustment.

## Ledger

The record of every entry the books hold, account by account.

## Net burn

The cash a month's operations and investments used, less the cash they brought in.

## Posting

Writing an entry into the ledger. A posted entry never changes: a mistake is corrected by a reversal,
so the history shows both.

## Prepayment

A cost paid in advance, such as a year's insurance, which becomes a cost month by month as the
company uses what it paid for.

## Reconciliation

Checking that the bank's lines and the books agree, line by line, so every amount in the bank is in
the books once.

## Reversal

An entry that undoes a posted entry exactly. Both stay in the books, so you can see what changed and
why.

## Runway

How many months the cash in the bank would last at the average net burn of the last three months.

## Suspense

The account (2999) where money waits until someone says what it is for. It should be empty when a
month is closed.

## Trial balance

Every account's balance at a date. Its debits equal its credits.
